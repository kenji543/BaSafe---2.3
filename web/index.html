How it works

From a point on the map to an understandable result

Every step keeps the original source evidence visible and treats unavailable data honestly.

  1. 1

    Select a Location

    Search for a place, enter coordinates, use your current location, or click the map.

  2. 2

    View Hazard Layers

    Examine available flood, liquefaction, and ground-shaking layers with legends and sources.

  3. 3

    Generate a Score

    Process valid source classifications through the documented fuzzy model.

  4. 4

    Understand the Result

    Review inputs, rules, warnings, recommendations, and the downloadable report.

How the score works

A relative 0–100 screening result — not a safety rating

The configured model combines only the required, valid hazard inputs. Interpretation bands describe relative screening concern and remain subject to model validation.

The score is a relative screening result based only on selected available hazard inputs. It is not a probability of disaster, a safety rating, or an official site certification.
0–20Very Low
21–40Low

Why the result is explainable

See how the result was produced

The score does not hide the evidence behind a single number.

Source classifications→ Normalized inputs→ Objective weights→ Fuzzy memberships→ Activated rules→ Screening result

Each score exposes source hazard values, normalized inputs, applied weights, membership categories, principal rules, missing-data conditions, the final category, and a plain-language explanation.

Frequently asked questions

Know what the system can — and cannot — tell you

Does Basafe determine whether a place is safe?

No. It provides a preliminary relative screening result and never declares a location safe or unsafe.

Where does the hazard information come from?

From selected configured GeoRiskPH services and appropriate datasets published by mandated agencies, with Basey context when permitted.

Why can some layers be unavailable?

A service may be unreachable, outside coverage, invalid, or not yet configured with a verified authorized endpoint.

What does the 0–100 score mean?

It is a relative screening output from the configured fuzzy model, not a probability, forecast, or safety grade.

Can the report be used for a building permit or property certification?

No. It is not an official hazard certification, zoning approval, engineering clearance, or permit recommendation.

Is an account required?

No. Core mapping, scoring, explanation, and report functions are public.

Can the application work offline?

The application shell and some previously viewed information may be available, but new hazard data and complete scores normally require a connection.

Who should users consult for an official assessment?

Consult the relevant mandated hazard agency and qualified planning, engineering, geological, or geotechnical professionals.
